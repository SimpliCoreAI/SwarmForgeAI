What it is

SwarmForge is an AI operations layer for independent hotels. It doesn't replace your PMS — it sits on top of it and runs the daily operating rhythm: guest messaging, tasks, goals, approvals, and reports, with a team of AI agents doing the heavy lifting.

The core idea is approval-gated AI: the AI drafts, proposes, and prepares — a human always approves. Nothing guest-facing goes out without a person saying yes.

Key concepts

  • AI desks — specialist agents (reputation, revenue, maintenance, and more) that work your goals in the background and bring you decisions, not raw data.
  • Ticks — the unit of AI work. Each hotel gets a weekly tick budget (e.g. 2 ticks/week for opportunity scans). Ticks keep AI costs predictable.
  • AI budget — a daily cap per hotel on AI spending. If a wave of agent work can't fit the budget, it waits instead of failing halfway.
  • Approval gates — routine work flows to staff, money and reputation decisions flow to the GM. You set the tiers.

Who it's for

  • Independent hotels and small groups (the product is built around properties like a 20–60 room hotel).
  • General Managers who want leverage, not another dashboard to babysit.
  • Owners who want portfolio visibility without asking the GM for reports.

What it is

SwarmForge has five roles. Each sees exactly what it needs — and nothing it shouldn't. Guest identity (names, phone numbers, message contents) is visible only to the GM.

The roles

General Manager (GM)

  • Full access to everything: all sections, approvals, guest identity, reports, setup.
  • The only role that can change team members' roles.
  • Business case: the GM is the single throat to choke — every money, reputation, and exception decision lands here, and nowhere else.

Manager

  • Sees operations with room numbers only — never guest names or phone numbers.
  • Can approve Tier-2 items (section-level decisions) and manage tasks in their section.
  • Business case: lets a floor manager run housekeeping or F&B for a shift without exposing guest PII.

Staff

  • Sees only their assigned tasks and requests they raised themselves.
  • In WhatsApp, sends AI-drafted replies for their section after review.
  • Business case: a receptionist can handle the day's check-ins and guest chats without seeing revenue data or other sections' work.

Owner

  • Sees aggregates and statistics only — revenue, occupancy, health scores. Never guest names, phones, or message contents.
  • Can assign goals to a hotel; they land on the GM's board with an "Assigned by owner" badge.
  • Business case: a portfolio owner checks all properties on Monday morning without a single phone call.

Ops (platform operator)

  • Provisions hotels, manages subscriptions, monitors sync health, and holds kill switches.
  • Every ops action is written to an append-only audit log.

How to use effectively

  1. Start minimal. Give the GM account first, then add one manager and the front-desk staff who'll actually send WhatsApp replies.
  2. Match roles to shifts. The person on the reception desk gets the Staff role with the Reception section — not Manager.
  3. Review quarterly. In Setup → Team, check the seat list whenever someone joins or leaves. Remove leavers the same day.
  4. Never share logins. The audit log records who approved what — shared logins destroy that accountability.

Setting up personas (how-to)

  1. As GM, go to Setup → Team.
  2. Click Invite, enter the person's email, and pick their role (GM / Manager / Staff).
  3. They receive an invitation and set their own password.
  4. To change someone's role later: Setup → Team → edit the member. Only the GM can do this — this is the guardrail against self-granted access.
Getting started Hotel setup guide Set up a property, import reservation data, configure policies, and launch a first goal. GmPlatform Ops +

What it is

Hotel setup is a 10-tab checklist in Setup that takes a property from "account created" to "running daily operations." Work through the tabs in order — each one unlocks the next layer of the product.

The 10 tabs

1. Subscription (Plans)

Confirms your tier and limits: room count, seats, documents/month, WhatsApp guests/month, and whether ancillary revenue is enabled. If you're near a cap, you'll see an upgrade nudge — WhatsApp guest messaging is never cut off mid-stay (it's a soft cap; the GM just gets notified).

2. Property identity

Hotel name, address, room count, and brand basics. The room count syncs to your subscription tier.

3. Website

Coming soon — this will hold your public booking-site settings.

4. PMS import

Connect your reservation data. Today this is a CSV upload (reservations and rooms); an eZee API sync is on the roadmap. Upload freshness matters — revenue reports and owner views show how stale the data is, so re-upload on a rhythm (weekly at minimum). Business case: every revenue, occupancy, and channel report in the product is only as fresh as this import.

5. Sellable extras

Define the ancillary services you sell beyond rooms — airport transfers, laundry, spa slots, late checkout. These power the Services section and upsell suggestions.

6. Rules

Operating rules the AI desks and staff follow: check-in/check-out times, quiet hours, comp policies, escalation paths.

7. Policies & documents

Upload policy documents (cancellation, pet policy, ID requirements). The AI references these when drafting guest replies so answers stay consistent.

8. Team

Invite staff by email and assign roles. See Roles & personas.

9. WhatsApp

Connect your business number, configure the section inbox (Reception, Housekeeping, F&B), set per-section SLA targets and staff assignments, and generate the guest opt-in QR code. Full walkthrough in WhatsApp setup.

10. First goal

Create your first AI goal so the desks have something to work on from day one. Good starters: "Lift review score above 4.5" or "Cut response time under 5 minutes."

How to use effectively

  • Do it in one sitting with the GM present. Identity, rules, and WhatsApp config all need GM decisions — don't start without them.
  • Import PMS data before inviting staff. Empty reports confuse people; a week of reservation history makes every screen come alive.
  • Set the First goal to something visible in 30 days. Early wins build trust in the AI desks.
Hotel operations Daily operations Work the daily brief, guest inbox, task board, requests, approvals, and guest spend. GmManagerStaff +

Home

What it is: the GM's morning view — today's arrivals/departures, occupancy, the decision queue (items waiting on you), and FYI cards.

Business case: replaces the 8 AM walkaround-and-ask. One screen answers "what needs me today?"

Use it effectively: clear the decision queue first, then scan FYI cards. If the Home feels crowded, tell us — we keep trimming it.

WhatsApp section inbox

What it is: guest chats routed into three sections — Reception, Housekeeping, F&B / Room Service. The AI drafts a reply; staff always press send. Guests see room numbers only on the staff side; guest identity stays GM-only.

Business case: one phone number for the whole hotel, but every message lands with the right team, with a draft reply already written and an SLA clock running.

Use it effectively:

  • Keep routing ON only for sections that are staffed — an unstaffed section with routing on is worse than routing off.
  • Set honest SLA targets (e.g. Reception 5 min, Housekeeping 15 min). The AI measures against them.
  • Staff: always read the draft before sending. The AI is good; it's not you.

Tasks

What it is: the hotel's task board — create, assign, and track work from open → in progress → done, grouped by category (including maintenance).

Business case: the leaking tap in 204 stops living in someone's head and starts living on a board with an owner and a due date.

Use it effectively: every task needs an owner and a due date. A task with neither is a wish.

Requests

What it is: requests staff raise that need a decision — a comp, a late checkout, a rate exception.

Business case: stops hallway decisions. The request, the context, and the approval live in one place with an audit trail.

Approvals

What it is: the pending-decision queue, grouped by type. Approvals are tiered so the GM doesn't gate routine work:

  • Tier 1 — routine: staff or the AI handles it (e.g. standard late checkout within policy).
  • Tier 2 — section: the manager approves (e.g. a housekeeping comp).
  • Tier 3 — GM: money, reputation, and exceptions (e.g. large comps, review responses).

Business case: the GM approves 10 things a day instead of 100 — and the 10 are the ones that matter.

Use it effectively: push as much as safely possible into Tier 1 and 2. Review the tier boundaries monthly — if the GM queue is always full, the tiers are too tight.

Guest spend

What it is: bill-photo capture and manual charge entry (F&B, extras). The GM sees guest identity + spend; managers see room-number-only; staff see nothing.

Business case: every charge gets captured at the point of sale with photo evidence — no more "we forgot to add the coffees."

Use it effectively: make photo capture the habit, not the exception. Set your comp-approval threshold (default ₹2,000, GM-only setting) — comps and voids above it need approval; legitimate big charges are never blocked.

Hotel operations AI goals, opportunities & the daily brief Set measurable goals, review AI suggestions, and scan the morning brief. GmOwner +

Goals

What it is: objectives you set ("Hold review score above 4.5", "Grow ancillary revenue 15%") that AI desks work in the background. Each desk is a specialist agent that gathers evidence, drafts actions, and brings you decisions to approve.

Business case: a GM can't watch reviews, rates, maintenance, and upsells simultaneously. Desks can — and they only interrupt you when there's a decision.

Use it effectively:

  • Write goals with a number and a date: "Cut average response time under 5 minutes by month-end" beats "improve service."
  • Use the New goal composer — title, description, target, KPI — and preview which desks will pick it up before saving.
  • Cap active goals at 3–5. Ten goals means no goal.

Opportunities

What it is: a weekly AI scan that surfaces suggestion cards — revenue you might be leaving on the table, cost leaks, review risks. Each card can be turned into a goal with one click (which then follows the normal approval flow).

Business case: the "I didn't know we could do that" engine. Independent hotels rarely have a revenue manager; this is the closest thing.

Use it effectively:

  • The scan runs on 2 ticks per week per hotel (max 8 cards/week) — it's deliberately throttled so suggestions stay high-signal.
  • Give feedback on cards (useful / not useful). The scan learns what matters to your property.
  • Suggestions never execute automatically. "Turn into goal" is always your call.

Daily brief

What it is: a morning briefing generated from overnight activity — arrivals, departures, open tasks, SLA breaches, review mentions, and what each desk did while you slept.

Business case: the 5-minute read that replaces 30 minutes of asking around.

Use it effectively: read it before the Home screen. If something in the brief needs action, it links straight to the task, approval, or chat.

Completed (archive)

What it is: a read-only archive of finished tasks and signed-off goals.

Business case: proof of work — for the GM's own review, for the owner, or for "when did we fix the boiler?" six months later. The GM can reopen an item if it regresses.

Hotel operations Services & ancillary revenue Configure sellable extras and review ancillary activity. GmManager +

What it is

The catalog of everything the hotel sells beyond the room — airport transfers, laundry, spa, late checkout, F&B extras — plus charge entry and capture statistics.

Business case: rooms have fixed inventory; ancillaries are where margin grows. Most independent hotels under-sell them simply because there's no system tracking them.

How to use effectively

  1. Build the catalog first (Setup → Sellable extras). Every item needs a name, price, and the team that fulfills it.
  2. Enter charges at the point of sale. F&B and extras should hit the system the moment they're served — see Guest spend.
  3. Watch the capture stats weekly. Which extras sell, which don't, and which rooms never buy anything — that's your upsell plan.
  4. Tie extras to the guest journey. Late checkout offered at check-in converts; offered never doesn't.

Notes

  • Ancillary features are enabled on Growth and Scale tiers (not Starter).
  • Local-business offers to guests (partner restaurants, experiences) are a separate planned feature — hotel ancillaries always get priority in guest messaging.
Reports & insights Reports & analytics Read revenue, task timing, health, and data-freshness reports. GmOwner +

What it is

Reports turn your reservation data and operational activity into numbers you can act on. Everything is computed from data already in the system — no separate BI tool needed.

Available reports

Revenue

Earnings, bookings, ADR (average daily rate), occupancy, and channel split (direct vs OTA). Filter by date range. Business case: "Are we making more money than last month, and why?" — answered in one screen.

Timings

Response times, resolution times, and SLA compliance per section. Business case: proves whether the WhatsApp inbox is actually faster than the front-desk phone — and where the bottlenecks are.

Hotel health score

A deterministic score from operational signals (occupancy, SLA compliance, review trends, task completion), with an AI-written narrative explaining it. Business case: one number for "how are we doing," with the story behind it.

Data freshness

Every report shows how fresh its data is. Reservation data comes from your PMS import (Setup → PMS import) — if the last upload was 3 weeks ago, the reports say so. Keep imports on a weekly rhythm, or connect the eZee API sync when available.

How to use effectively

  • Monday routine: revenue + health score, 10 minutes. That's the week-in-review.
  • Monthly: channel split. If OTA share is climbing, that's a direct-booking problem to fix.
  • Never screenshot a stale report into a decision. Check the freshness badge first.
Reports & insights Owner view & Ops console Review portfolio health as an owner or manage hotel provisioning and platform operations. OwnerPlatform Ops +

Owner view

What it is: a portfolio dashboard at /owner for hotel owners — revenue, bookings, ADR, occupancy, channel data, and health scores across their properties.

Privacy by design: owners see aggregates and statistics only — never guest names, phone numbers, message contents, or identity. Every owner–hotel link is operator-verified (recorded with who verified it and when); there is no self-service hotel claiming.

Business case: the Monday-morning portfolio check without calling a single GM.

Features:

  • Per-hotel health scores with AI narratives.
  • Revenue and occupancy from reservation data, with upload-freshness shown per hotel (stale data is visible, not hidden).
  • Owner-assigned goals: an owner can assign a goal to a hotel; it lands on the GM's board with an "Assigned by owner" badge.

Use it effectively: sort by freshness first — a hotel with 3-week-old data needs a nudge before its numbers mean anything.

Ops console

What it is: the platform operator's back office at /ops — hotel provisioning, subscription management, sync monitoring, and safety controls.

Features:

  • Provisioning: create the hotel, the GM account, and the tier. The GM gets a temp password (shown once) and must change it at first login.
  • Hotel disable: a full soft stop — logins rejected, sessions killed, scheduled work skipped, WhatsApp in/out stopped. Data untouched; re-enable restores everything.
  • Kill switches: per-hotel WhatsApp routing toggle, AI pause for billing issues.
  • Audit logs: every ops action (logins, approvals, deletions, disables) in an append-only log kept indefinitely. Hotel actions are separately visible to that hotel's GM.
  • AI budget caps: per-hotel daily AI spend limits; waves of agent work reserve budget atomically so a run either fully launches or cleanly waits.

Use it effectively: destructive actions (disable/delete) require the ops action password and a confirmation — they're irreversible by design. Kill switches are reversible and need no password.

Setup & integrations WhatsApp setup & configuration Connect a business number, route sections, set SLAs, and configure guest opt-in. GmManagerStaffPlatform Ops +

What it is

SwarmForge runs guest messaging on WhatsApp through Meta's official Cloud API — one business number per hotel, with a section-routed inbox (Reception, Housekeeping, F&B / Room Service). The AI drafts replies; staff always send.

Setup steps

1. Business number

Each hotel needs a dedicated business phone number. Important: registering a number with WhatsApp Business disconnects it from the WhatsApp phone/desktop apps — plan for a second number if the front desk relies on the app today.

2. Meta onboarding

  • A WhatsApp Business Account (WABA) holds all hotel numbers.
  • Each display name needs Meta approval; business verification happens once at the account level.
  • Your ops team completes Meta setup and stores the API token server-side — tokens are never entered in the app UI.

3. Connect in SwarmForge

In Setup → WhatsApp (GM only):

  • Enter the hotel's Meta phone number ID.
  • Turn routing on/off per hotel (ships OFF — enable when staff are ready).
  • Set per-section SLA targets and staff assignments.
  • Generate the guest opt-in QR code for the front desk.

4. Guest opt-in

Guests opt in by scanning the QR at check-in. Consent is logged with timestamp and source; guests can revoke with STOP at any time. Rules: no messages after checkout, max 1–2 guest messages per day, no bill integration (settled at front desk).

5. Message templates

Proactive messages (booking confirmations, check-in instructions) use Meta-approved templates. Your ops team manages template approval.

Business case

Guests already live on WhatsApp. One hotel number with the right team answering in minutes — with AI-drafted replies — beats a front-desk phone that rings out, and every conversation is logged against the stay.

How to use effectively

  • Staff the sections before enabling routing. An SLA clock running on an empty section just creates breaches.
  • Keep the human in the loop. The AI drafts; staff send. Skim every draft — tone matters.
  • Broadcast is separate. Bulk/announcement messaging is a planned separate feature, not part of the inbox.

Ticks

AI budget

Dunning (failed payments)

  • Day 0: account flagged (amber), you're notified.
  • Day 7: AI features pause until payment resumes.
  • Day 14: subscription ends locally. Your hotel data, logins, and WhatsApp history remain intact — nothing is deleted.
Troubleshooting FAQ & troubleshooting Find quick fixes for access, WhatsApp, reports, AI features, and billing. All roles +

Login & access

I can't log in. Check you're on the right domain (app vs demo). If you were provisioned by ops, use the temp password from your invite — you'll be forced to change it on first login. Still stuck? Ask your GM to confirm your account is active in Setup → Team.

I see a different hotel's data / wrong role. Roles are GM-managed (Setup → Team). Only the GM can change roles. If your access looks wrong, ask your GM — support can't reassign roles for you.

WhatsApp

Guest messages aren't arriving. Check Setup → WhatsApp: is routing ON for the hotel? Is the Meta phone number ID set? Is the section staffed and assigned? Routing ships OFF — it must be deliberately enabled.

The AI draft looks wrong. Edit before sending — staff always send. If drafts are consistently off, check Setup → Policies & documents (the AI drafts from your policies) and Rules.

A guest says STOP but still gets messages. Consent revocation is immediate in the system. If a message was already queued before the STOP, it may still deliver once. Report repeats to support.

Data & reports

Reports look stale. Check the freshness badge on the report. Reservation data comes from Setup → PMS import — re-upload your CSV (weekly rhythm recommended).

My CSV upload failed. Files over 1MB are rejected, as are iPhone HEIC photos and Word docs for bill evidence (use PNG/JPG/PDF). Check the error message — it tells you which rule tripped.

AI features

"Low AI budget — desks couldn't run." The hotel hit its daily AI cap. Briefs are retained and the wave will run after the cap resets (or ask ops about raising it).

Opportunity cards stopped appearing. The scan runs weekly on 2 ticks/hotel. If the hotel's tick or AI budget is exhausted, the scan waits. Check Setup → Subscription for usage vs caps.

Billing

What happens if we miss a payment? Day 0: flagged, you're notified. Day 7: AI pauses. Day 14: subscription ends locally — data, logins, and WhatsApp history are kept.

Reports & insights How metrics are calculated See the formulas, freshness rules, and role visibility behind SwarmForge metrics. GmManagerStaffOwner +

Every number in SwarmForge is measured from your hotel's own data — never modeled, never borrowed from industry benchmarks. This article documents exactly how each metric is computed, grouped by who sees it. If a number isn't listed here, SwarmForge doesn't compute it.

For owners

Health score (0–100)

Each hotel gets one score, recomputed daily.

  • Formula: every factor produces a sub-score from 0–100; the hotel score is the weighted average of the factors that have data. If no factor has data, the score is 50 (neutral).
  • The seven factors and their weights:
FactorWeightWhat it measures
Approval responsiveness20%Pending approvals: how many, and their average age in hours
SLA health20%Share of tasks past their due time
Issue-resolution time15%Average hours from task creation to completion (last 7 days)
Occupancy15%This week's room-nights vs the prior four-week average
AI handling rate10%Share of guest messages drafted by AI vs sent by staff (last 7 days)
Goal progress10%Share of goal-linked tasks done or awaiting approval (last 30 days)
GM engagement10%Recency of the GM's own activity in the system
  • Bands: 85+ Thriving · 70–84 Healthy · 50–69 Needs attention · below 50 At risk.
  • Freshness cap: the score trusts your bookings data. If bookings were never uploaded — or are 14+ days old — the score caps at 69; 7–13 days old caps at 84. Under 7 days, no cap.
  • Trend arrow: compares today's score with the previous scored day. Moves under 3 points show as flat.
  • History: 90 days of daily scores are kept, so trends are real history, not estimates.
  • The written summary under the score is AI-generated from these same factors.

Return on AI

Available for this month, the last 30 days, or year to date.

  • Incremental revenue: charges stamped to an AI offer when the upsell is fulfilled, summed from your ledger. Measured, never modeled. It reads ₹0 until staff post AI-offer charges from the app.
  • AI cost: settled AI spend for the period, metered in USD. Ticks only — subscription fees are excluded. For the multiple, USD is converted at a fixed ₹94/USD display rate.
  • Multiple: incremental revenue ÷ AI cost, shown like "3.2×". If AI cost is zero it shows "—", never infinity. The portfolio multiple is computed from portfolio totals.
  • Lever cards (each names the feature driving it): Ancillary uplift = incremental revenue (AI upsell offers over WhatsApp) · Response quality = average first-response minutes (WhatsApp section inbox + AI drafts) · Recovered revenue = count of acted-on revenue opportunities — a count, never rupees · AI cost (AI desks, tick-metered).
  • Labor saved is Coming Soon. No modeled numbers appear anywhere.
  • Baselines: each hotel's first 30 days on SwarmForge. Trends compare the selected period against the previous equivalent period.

Revenue stats

Per hotel: Earnings = sum of reservation amounts · Bookings = count · ADR = earnings ÷ room nights · Occupancy = room nights ÷ (rooms × days), capped at 100% · Direct share = direct bookings ÷ total bookings. A freshness badge shows how old the bookings upload is ("Data today", "Stale · 9 d ago", "No upload yet").

Goal progress

Owner-assigned goals land on the GM's board automatically. Progress reads "N of M tasks done" — linked hotel tasks in done/verified status. If there are no linked tasks yet, it shows nothing rather than a guess.

For GMs

Daily Brief

Covers the window since you last opened the brief, up to 24 hours back. Sections: approvals waiting (newest first), approvals you decided, tasks completed, guest requests resolved, arrivals and checkouts, anomalies (overdue and blocked tasks), top spenders and revenue at risk (by outstanding balance — GM only), trailing-24h ancillary revenue by category, a 30-day ancillary chart, and year-so-far totals. Each section is independent: if one fails to load, the rest still render.

Tasks

Lifecycle: proposed → open → in progress → done → verified (blocked and rejected are side states). Overdue = past its due time and not done/verified. Maintenance requests at or above your cost threshold (default ₹2,000, you set it) need your approval; below it a manager can approve; housekeeping requests open immediately. The Timings tab (GM only) shows approval wait, work time, and total time per task kind, derived from task history.

Guest tiers & SLAs

  • Silver: 1+ stays · Gold: 2+ stays · Diamond: suite stay or high spend (default ₹25,000) · Platinum: manual assignment only — never automatic.
  • Routing: Platinum → you + the department · Diamond/Gold → manager + staff · Silver → staff.
  • Priority: safety keywords (fire, smoke, gas leak, medical, emergency…) always escalate immediately, regardless of tier. Otherwise Platinum complaints and requests are top priority; everything else steps down by tier.
  • SLAs: urgent 2 min · standard 10 min · routine 15 min (you can edit these in Setup). Unacknowledged urgent messages escalate one level up after 5 minutes.
  • Any role may assign tiers; every change is audit-logged. Muting a guest silences notifications only — messages still arrive.

AI usage (ticks)

  • One tick = one run of the main AI agent. Specialist desks draw from the AI budget without consuming ticks.
  • Allowances: Starter 4 ticks/day · Growth 12/day · Scale 36/day; monthly AI budgets of $5 / $25 / $80. Prices: $3 per million prompt tokens, $15 per million completion tokens.
  • At the limit the AI pauses politely instead of spending — nothing fails halfway. /ops can grant top-up credit instantly ($1–$500).
  • Usage vs allowance is visible in the app header — GM only. If AI is paused for a billing issue, logins, data, and guest WhatsApp messaging keep working.

For managers

You see the same operations metrics as the GM — task board, brief sections, room-readiness exceptions, ancillary performance — with three deliberate limits:

  1. No guest identity. Names, phone numbers, and message contents are GM-only. Spend shows as room-number totals.
  2. No request-timings tab and no team management. Those are GM-only.
  3. Approvals: you resolve routine sub-threshold items; anything at or above the GM's cost threshold routes to the GM.

You can verify completed tasks and work your assigned WhatsApp sections. The AI usage chip and budget figures are not shown to managers.

For staff

Staff see their own work only — no dashboard metrics, no spend browsing, no approvals:

  • Assigned tasks (you move the status on your own tasks; you can't verify or block others').
  • WhatsApp sections you're assigned to (room numbers only, never guest identity).
  • F&B charge posting with a bill photo.

There are no per-staff scores or rankings in the hotel app. (Per-staff productivity stats are a Coming Soon item on the owner's Team tab — nothing is fabricated in the meantime.)

What SwarmForge does not compute

To be explicit — these do not exist as metrics, even if the words appear in passing:

  • Upsell attach rate and ancillary revenue per occupied room (no formula exists yet)
  • Room-readiness risk scores
  • Approval aging or SLA metrics on the approval queue
  • Per-staff productivity scores
  • Modeled labor savings or industry-benchmark comparisons presented as your data

If it's not measured from your ledger, your tasks, or your messages, it doesn't appear as a number.
