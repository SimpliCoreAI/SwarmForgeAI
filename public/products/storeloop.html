StoreLoop

The store, counted. The cost, visible.

Inventory control for the hotel store. It replaces the paper requisition with a digital one, reconciled against counts from the phone — so the GM sees what moved, what it cost, and what to buy next.

Why it exists

The store runs on paper: a requisition sheet, a phone count, a bill in a folder. Nobody can say, at the end of the week, what left the store or what it cost. StoreLoop replaces the paper, not the people. The kitchen raises, the GM approves, the store issues, and the count closes the loop.

What's in it

Digital requisition

Raise from the phone. The from-store is picked at raise time, so the balance shown is the right store's balance.

Counts from the phone

Count sheets print blind. The count goes in on the phone, against the requisition it closes.

Variance the GM can see

What was issued against what was counted, with the difference visible — not buried in a register.

Daily digest

One short WhatsApp summary each morning: what moved, what is low, what needs a decision.

Uploads

Paper sheets and bills photographed from the phone into one folder per hotel, grouped by category: Requisition, Invoice/bill, Challan, Count sheet, Market list, Other.

Printable forms

The requisition prints cleanly for the file. Prices and variance print only for roles allowed to see them.
How it works, end to end

Raise → approve → issue → count → variance → digest. AskAI proposes restocks and explains variance. A person approves. StoreLoop never orders, never pays, and never adjusts stock on its own.

The rules

  • StoreLoop touches no guest data. It is the store, not the guest.
  • Prices and variance are visible only to roles allowed to see them.
  • Every movement is recorded — who moved it, when, and against which requisition.

Starting with

The kitchen store first, for 30 days. Liquor and minibar follow once the kitchen loop is proven.

What it is not

  • Not a PMS, POS, accounting, or procurement system. It reads the store; it does not become the ledger.
  • Not food-cost control on day one. Without a POS link, it shows variance and movement — not a food-cost percentage.
